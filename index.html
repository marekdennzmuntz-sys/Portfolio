About

Most marketing treats content as output. I build it as infrastructure.

I'm a Nairobi-based brand and digital strategist. My background is in service and experiential marketing — travel, careers, consumer brands — and I currently lead digital brand for Zenith Steel Fabricators, applying the same systems thinking to a technical, B2B category.

Across every category, the pattern repeats. Campaigns rarely fail because the ideas are weak. They fail because nobody built the system connecting objective, relationship, message, and sequence — so activity quietly substitutes for movement. That's where I bring the most value to brands.

What most marketing runs on

  • A content calendar standing in for a strategy
  • Posts judged one at a time, by isolated engagement
  • Persuasion — ask people to believe the claim
  • A campaign that ends when the announcement does

What I build instead

  • A system: objective → relationship → message → sequence → response
  • Assets that have to answer four questions before they ship
  • Participation — give people something to experience first
  • A reveal designed as a bridge to what comes next

The distinction isn't stylistic. Every case study on this site is that argument, tested against a real brief — read them as evidence, not just examples.

The method

Strategy that stays honest.

Good marketing is not a volume exercise. It is an argument for why the brand needs to say what, what the audience is ready to hear, and what must happen next.

01

Read the signal

Find the tension between the brief, audience psychology, decision context, brand voice, competitive pressure, and the emotional or transactional engagement.

02

Build the system

Translate the insight into positioning, message architecture, campaign phases, content pillars, creative roles, channel logic, and practical delivery tools.

03

Make it travel

Deploy content and campaign assets across the touchpoints where the audience actually forms, moves, and can act on belief.

04

Learn forward

Use response, engagement, and conversation to make the next decision less about a creative instinct, and more about an afterthought.
