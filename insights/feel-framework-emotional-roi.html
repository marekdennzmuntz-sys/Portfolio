Most brands treat emotional experience as a soft cost — a nice-to-have that shows up in a satisfaction survey and stops there. It isn't soft. It shows up in four measurable dimensions of the business you're already tracking: how much it costs to serve someone, how much they spend, whether they bring others, and whether they stay.

F — Friction reduction

Positive emotion lowers the cost of doing business — measurably.

What to track

Cost per complaint before vs. after an experience intervention. Churn rate segmented by touchpoint satisfaction score. Support ticket volume per customer cohort. Average resolution time by interaction quality.

ROI signal

A 20% drop in escalations after service retraining is a direct, attributable cost saving with a clear emotional cause.

E — Expansion revenue

Customers who feel seen spend more, upgrade more, resist competitors more.

What to track

Net Revenue Retention — is each customer worth more this year than last? Upsell/cross-sell conversion by satisfaction tier. Average order value trend post-experience touchpoint. Repeat purchase frequency by emotional feedback score.

ROI signal

Customers rating experience 9–10 show 34% higher average order value than those rating 6–7. That gap is your emotional premium.

E — Evangelism value

Word of mouth is the overflow of emotional surplus. It is calculable.

What to track

NPS tracked as a revenue predictor, not a vanity metric. Referral conversion rate and attributed revenue. Share of organic vs. paid acquisition over time. CLV of referred customers vs. paid-acquired customers.

ROI signal

Referred customers show 16–25% higher CLV than paid-acquired customers. That gap is the monetary value of one emotional advocate.

L — Loyalty premium

Price sensitivity is largely emotional. Connection is a revenue hedge.

What to track

Price elasticity by customer tenure and satisfaction tier. Churn rate vs. industry benchmark — the gap is your retention advantage. Win-back cost for emotionally disconnected customers. Willingness-to-pay index by engagement tier.

ROI signal

Top-tier churn rate vs. bottom-tier, multiplied by average contract value, gives you an actual dollar figure for emotional connection.

"Stop measuring satisfaction. Start measuring surplus."

From signal to dollar figure.

Put together, the four dimensions collapse into one calculation: Emotional ROI equals the revenue and cost delta between your high- and low-engagement tiers, divided by the cost of the experience investment that moved people between them.

The question worth sitting with isn't whether emotion matters — it's which of the four dimensions your brand currently has no data for at all. Friction, expansion, evangelism, loyalty: pick the one you couldn't currently put a number on, and start there.
