The audit before the strategy.

A strong product with real word-of-mouth still needs a digital presence that reflects it. This is a sample audit and roadmap for Maji Magic — Africa's first inflatable aqua park — showing how I read a brand before I write the brief.

Maji Magic bills itself as Africa's first inflatable aqua park, and the reviews back the excitement up — a strong 4.4-star average across roughly 742 Google reviews, with TripAdvisor and other listings confirming the same family-friendly, safety-first reputation. That's a genuinely rare starting point: real product-market fit, real word of mouth. The question an audit exists to answer is whether the digital presence is doing that reputation justice.

Website

majimagic.com presents the offer clearly with contact and booking details, but leans on limited SEO content, routes bookings through third-party platforms, and was slow to load on mobile. No gallery, FAQ, or storytelling content to attract organic search traffic.

Social

Active handles across Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and LinkedIn (around 1,000 followers), but posting is sporadic — some content over eight months old — with no real content strategy: no UGC push, no storytelling, no brand collaborations.

Reviews & reputation

The strongest asset in the audit — a 4.4-star average that confirms genuine customer satisfaction — undercut by weak review management: few visible responses, and no structured system for asking happy visitors to leave one.

The SWOT

A unique offering that hasn't yet found its digital match.

Strengths

A unique offering with no direct local equivalent. Strong reputation (4.4+ across hundreds of reviews). Good engagement potential across both family and group-event audiences.

Weaknesses

Inconsistent social content. Limited SEO and no direct online booking. No clear digital campaign or influencer engagement strategy.

Opportunities

Group packages and occasion-based offers. Partnerships with travel influencers. Brand collaborations that extend reach into adjacent audiences.

Threats

Seasonal dips on weekdays. Reputational risk from an unmanaged negative review. Competitors gaining ground digitally while the category's leader stays quiet online.

The 6-month roadmap.

An audit is only useful if it turns into a sequence. The proposed roadmap moved in four phases, each building on the credibility the last one established — the same "system, not a burst" logic behind every content plan on this site.

The working sequence

Foundation first. Then content. Then offers. Then retention.

Months 1–2

Foundation & brand refresh

SEO-targeted website pages, a visible booking flow, an FAQ and safety section, and analytics installed so every later decision has data behind it.

Months 3–4

Content acceleration

A minimum three-posts-a-week cadence, a consistent hashtag system, and 5–10 nano and micro influencer partnerships offering free passes for content rights.

Month 5

Promotions & offers

Weekday deals and school-group packages to fix the seasonal dip, seasonal events for repeat occasions, and an email list to start owning the relationship directly.

Month 6

Leverage reviews & retargeting

A structured ask-for-reviews campaign, retargeting ads for warm website visitors, and partnership growth with travel agencies, hotels, and corporate team-building buyers.

The roadmap was built against explicit targets, not vague ambition: a 50% lift in website bookings by month six, 200% growth in social engagement, a 20% increase in repeat visitors, and 5,000 combined new followers across platforms. Whether or not a plan like this ultimately runs, naming the targets up front is what makes a roadmap accountable rather than aspirational.

"Maji Magic has a unique product with strong word-of-mouth and excellent reviews, but its digital presence doesn't yet reflect its real-world excitement."

What a sample audit is for.

This one isn't included here as a client result — it's included because it's a clean, complete example of the first thing I actually do before any strategy gets written: read the website, read the socials, read the reviews, and be honest about the gap between the real-world reputation and what a stranger finds online. Everything upstream of a good campaign depends on getting that diagnosis right.
